Chhattisgarh

Land of Ancient Tribes & Lost Kingdoms

Explore a rich heritage of ancient temples, formidable forts, and the grand palaces of a land steeped in history.

The Forts of Chhattisgarh

The forts of Chhattisgarh are powerful reminders of the region's past, built by dynasties like the Kalachuris to defend their territories and project their power.

Ratanpur Fort

Once the capital of the Kalachuri dynasty, this ancient fort near Bilaspur is a site of immense historical significance. Though now in a dilapidated state, its ruins, including exquisite rock carvings and grand gateways like the Singh Dwar, showcase the architectural brilliance of its builders. [7, 8, 9, 10, 11]

Chaiturgarh Fort (Lafagarh)

Perched at an altitude of over 3,000 feet, Chaiturgarh is considered one of India's strongest natural forts. Its defenses rely on the formidable cliffs that surround it. Built by the Kalachuri dynasty, it houses the famous Mahishasur Mardini temple and offers breathtaking views of the landscape. [12, 13, 14, 15, 16]

Temples & Monuments

Chhattisgarh is a treasure trove of ancient temples, showcasing a rich history of religious art and architecture from the Gupta period to the reign of the Kalachuris.

Bhoramdeo Temple Complex

Famously known as the "Khajuraho of Chhattisgarh," this 11th-century temple complex is dedicated to Lord Shiva. Its walls are adorned with stunningly detailed and erotic sculptures, similar to those at Khajuraho, representing a pinnacle of the Nagara style of architecture in the region. [7, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21]

Devrani-Jethani Temples, Tala

These 5th-6th century temples are among the earliest in the region. The site is famous for the excavation of a unique and colossal Rudra-Shiva sculpture, a masterpiece of Gupta-era art where the deity's form is composed of various animals and human heads. [7, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26]

Palaces of the Princely States

The palaces of Chhattisgarh reflect the grandeur of its former princely states, blending traditional Indian designs with colonial-era architectural styles.

Bastar Palace, Jagdalpur

The historic seat of the rulers of the Bastar kingdom, this palace is a beautiful example of regional architecture. It served as the administrative center and royal residence after the capital was shifted from Barsur. Today, it houses a museum showcasing the region's rich tribal and royal heritage. [7, 22, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31]

Kanker Palace

Built in the early 20th century, this palace showcases an elegant Colonial style of architecture. Once the residence of the British Agent, it is now a heritage hotel run by the royal family, offering visitors a glimpse into the regal lifestyle of a bygone era. [7, 32, 33, 34, 35]

When to Visit

Chhattisgarh has a tropical climate with hot summers and a pleasant winter. The best time to explore its historical sites is during the cooler, drier months.

Peak Season (October - March)

The winter months offer the most pleasant weather, with mild temperatures and clear skies, making it perfect for visiting temples, forts, and the state's famous waterfalls. [36, 37]

Monsoon Season (July - September)

The monsoon brings lush greenery to the landscape, but heavy rainfall can make travel to remote archaeological sites difficult. [37]

Off-Season (April - June)

Summers are extremely hot and humid. This period is generally challenging for tourism and extensive outdoor sightseeing. [37]
