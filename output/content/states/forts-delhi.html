The Forts of Delhi

Delhi's forts are not just defensive structures; they were entire walled cities that served as the political and cultural heart of vast empires.

Red Fort (Lal Qila)

The zenith of Mughal power, the Red Fort was built by Emperor Shah Jahan in the 17th century as the palace-fort of his new capital, Shahjahanabad. This UNESCO World Heritage site, with its massive red sandstone walls, housed palaces, audience halls, and gardens, and remains a potent symbol of India's sovereignty.

Purana Qila (Old Fort)

One of the oldest forts in Delhi, its current form was built by Sher Shah Suri and the Mughal emperor Humayun. Believed to be located on the site of the ancient city of Indraprastha from the Mahabharata, its massive gateways and walls represent an earlier, more rugged style of Sultanate architecture.

Iconic Monuments

Delhi is home to some of the most significant monuments in India, showcasing the transition from the early Sultanate period to the high refinement of the Mughal era.

Qutb Minar Complex

A UNESCO World Heritage site, this complex marks the arrival of the Delhi Sultanate. The towering Qutb Minar, a 73-meter-high victory tower, and the Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque, the first mosque in Delhi, are prime examples of early Indo-Islamic architecture, famously using materials from demolished temples.

Humayun's Tomb

Another UNESCO site, this magnificent garden-tomb was commissioned in the 16th century and was the first of its kind on the Indian subcontinent. Its perfect symmetry, use of red sandstone and white marble, and grand *charbagh* garden set the architectural precedent for the Taj Mahal.

The Architecture of the Raj

After the capital of British India was shifted to Delhi in 1911, a new imperial city was built. Designed by Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker, this architecture was meant to symbolize the power and permanence of the British Empire.

Rashtrapati Bhavan (Viceroy's House)

The centerpiece of Lutyens' Delhi, this monumental palace was the residence of the Viceroy of India. A masterpiece of symmetry and scale, it blends Western classical design with Indian motifs like Buddhist railings and temple bells, and is now the official residence of the President of India.

India Gate

This 42-meter-high archway is a war memorial dedicated to the soldiers of British India who died in World War I. Designed by Lutyens, its triumphal arch form is a powerful and iconic landmark at the heart of the city's ceremonial axis, Rajpath.

When to Visit

Delhi experiences extreme weather, with very hot summers and cold winters. The best time to explore its many outdoor monuments is during the pleasant spring and autumn months.

Peak Season (October - March)

The winter months offer cool, sunny days, perfect for sightseeing. This is the most popular tourist season, with pleasant weather for exploring forts and tombs.

Monsoon Season (July - September)

The monsoon brings relief from the heat but also high humidity and heavy rain, which can disrupt travel and sightseeing plans.

Off-Season (April - June)

Summers are extremely hot, with temperatures often exceeding 40°C (104°F). It is advisable to avoid this period for extensive outdoor activities.
